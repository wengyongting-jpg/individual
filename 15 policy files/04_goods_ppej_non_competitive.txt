SYNTHCORP PROCUREMENT POLICY — GOODS: NON-COMPETITIVE PURCHASES AND THE PPEJ
STATUS: SYNTHETIC DOCUMENT FOR ACADEMIC PROTOTYPE ONLY.
This document is fictional and was created for the PE6201 End-of-Course Project. It does not represent
the real policy of any real institution or organization. Any resemblance to real procurement rules is
coincidental and for demonstration purposes only.

This document explains when a non-competitive (sole-source) goods purchase may be considered, and
the Procurement Policy Exemption Justification (PPEJ) process that supports it.

WHEN NON-COMPETITIVE PROCUREMENT MAY BE CONSIDERED
A non-competitive procurement may be considered only when a genuine competitive process is not
possible or not practical — for example, when there is only one supplier in the market reasonably
capable of providing the goods.

WHEN A PPEJ IS REQUIRED
Whenever the total procurement value exceeds $25,000 and a competitive process is not used, a
Procurement Policy Exemption Justification (PPEJ) is required. The PPEJ must explain, in sufficient
detail, why competition was not possible and what alternatives were considered, so that the decision
can be understood and reviewed later.

PPEJ RETENTION AND APPROVAL BY VALUE
- $25,000 to $74,999: the completed PPEJ must be retained in the department's procurement file.
- $75,000 or more: the PPEJ must be reviewed and approved by the Contracting Authority before the
  department commits to the recommended supplier.

LIMITS ON USING AN EXCEPTION
A procurement exception must never be used simply to avoid competition, and must never be used to
favour a particular supplier without genuine justification. Doing so is a compliance violation.
Non-competitive procurements are subject to internal audit review and may be subject to information
requests.
